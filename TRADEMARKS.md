# Trademarks and project name

This is a short, plain-language note about the **Requivo name and identity**. It is not legal advice
and does not create any right or obligation beyond what applicable law already provides.

> "Requivo" is used as the name of the official project. This document does not claim a registered
> trademark unless explicitly stated — and it is **not** currently claimed as a registered trademark.

## Code vs. name

The Requivo **code** is licensed under the [MIT License](LICENSE). MIT gives you broad freedom to
use, modify, distribute and even sell the code.

The MIT license covers the code **only**. It does not, by itself, grant permission to use the
**Requivo name, logo, visual identity, or official domains** in a way that implies your version is
the official project or is endorsed or supported by it.

## Forks are welcome

- You may fork the project and build on it — that is the point of open source.
- You may factually state that your work is **"based on Requivo"** or **"a fork of Requivo"**.
- A **substantially modified** fork should use its **own distinct name**, rather than presenting
  itself as "Requivo", to avoid confusing users about its origin or who supports it.
- Please do not use the Requivo name or logo in a way that could mislead people into thinking a fork,
  product, or service is the official Requivo, or is backed by its maintainer, when it is not.

## Official identity

The following are considered part of the official project's identity: the **Requivo** name, its logo
and visual identity (if/when published), and its official domains and accounts (if/when reserved).
Using the code under MIT does not transfer these.

## Questions

If you're unsure whether a particular use of the name is fine, open an issue and ask. The intent here
is simply to keep "Requivo" meaning *the one project*, not to restrict genuine open-source use of the
code.
